# Candidate Acceptance Checklist

Compare the candidate with the frozen source. A stylistic impression never
overrides semantic fidelity.

## Hard gates

Mark `FAIL` if any answer is no:

1. Are all facts, numbers, units, equations, citations, labels, and terms
   preserved?
2. Are negation, modality, comparison, causal direction, and claim strength
   unchanged?
3. Are headings, numbered items, paragraph order, and paragraph roles intact?
4. Is every added phrase entailed by the source rather than invented context?
5. Does the candidate remain in the source's disciplinary register?

Any `FAIL` requires a local revert or rejection of the candidate.

## Quality review

Rate each item `0` (worse), `1` (unchanged), or `2` (clear improvement):

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Specificity | Are subjects and actions more concrete without adding facts? |
| Syntax | Is genuine local repetition reduced without random variation? |
| Flow | Are necessary logical relations clear without extra scaffolding? |
| Density | Is explanation proportional to the existing argument? |
| Register | Does it still sound like the relevant academic/technical genre? |
| Terminology | Are core names stable rather than varied for appearance? |
| Read-aloud quality | Does it read naturally without colloquial filler? |

Accept only when all hard gates pass and the candidate has a real net benefit.
The unchanged source remains a valid and often preferable choice.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- Do not alter a sentence merely because it contains a common transition.
- Do not force a three-item list into two or four items.
- Do not vary sentence length to meet a numerical target.
- Do not delete a conclusion based on its opening phrase.
- Do not use a detector percentage or this checklist score as proof of
  authorship, originality, or external acceptance.
